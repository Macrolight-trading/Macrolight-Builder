**A Troy, MI service-business website should make it easy to verify three things: you do the job the customer needs, you serve their address or have an office they can visit, and they can reach you without guessing what happens next.** Start with the page customers already land on, whether that’s a service page from Google Search or the page linked from your Google Business Profile. The homepage isn’t always the problem.

A dental practice in a building on Big Beaver Road can use its location page to show the suite, entrance, and parking instructions next to the booking button. An HVAC company that dispatches into Troy from elsewhere in Oakland County can use its furnace repair page to explain coverage, when the phone is staffed, and how a service request gets confirmed. It doesn’t need to pretend it has an office in Troy.

Before committing to a redesign, follow one of those paths from your phone. If you can find the right page but can’t complete a booking or quote request, fix that path first. If customers can’t find the page at all, check search visibility before changing the design.

## What should customers see before they scroll?

**Name the service, say how the customer gets it, and put the right action next to that information.** If you’re open for urgent calls, show a tappable number. If the customer needs an appointment or an estimate, use a button that says which one they’re requesting.

| Type of appointment or job | What to put near the button | What should happen next |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Office visit | Troy street address and suite; whether you take walk-ins; **Book an appointment** | Confirmation shows the address, entrance, and any parking instructions |
| Urgent service call | Actual dispatch hours; the work you take on; **Call for service** | The caller reaches a person or hears an accurate after-hours message |
| Planned on-site work | Where you work and what you need to price the job; **Request an estimate** | The customer knows who will follow up and whether a visit has been booked |

For planned work, ask only for details that affect the next step. A contractor might need the ZIP code, job type, and a callback number before scheduling a site visit. An office booking flow can make arrival details clear without asking a prospective customer to repeat information the scheduler already collected.

## When does a business need its own Troy page?

**Build a dedicated Troy page when you have something local that helps the customer make a decision.** A real office, a different set of services or appointment hours, or a documented local project can justify the page. If only the city name changes, keep one useful service page instead.

A Troy-area contractor who handles permitting could explain what the crew checks before quoting, who handles the application if one is needed, and whether permit costs are included in the estimate. Confirm the job address is within city limits before sending a customer to [Troy’s Building Department permit information](https://troymi.gov/departments/building_inspections/permit_requirements.php). Link to the city rather than copying rules that can change or implying every job needs the same permit.

Google’s [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies#doorway-abuse) flag near-identical location pages that funnel readers elsewhere. And an Oakland County service-area business should not set up a Troy listing just because it serves the city. [Google’s Business Profile guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) say businesses that go to customers and do not receive them at their address should hide that address. Google also says [local results](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091) are mainly based on relevance, distance, and prominence. Accurate coverage helps people decide; it doesn’t change where your business is located. Our [Business Profile guide](/blog/google-business-profile-optimization-2026) covers how to check the listing alongside the site.

## How should a Troy service page answer buying questions for search and AI?

**Ask your dispatcher or front desk which questions people ask before they book, then answer those questions on the relevant page.** Put the short answer before the explanation. Use visible text for coverage, availability, and what an estimate includes so customers don’t have to call just to learn whether the service fits.

For example, “Do you repair furnaces in Troy or only install them?” belongs on a furnace repair page. A specific answer can name what you repair, when you take calls, and how you confirm a visit. Use the words your team would use with a customer, not a list of neighboring cities. [Google’s AI search guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) emphasizes crawlable, indexed content that can appear with a search snippet; meeting the requirements does not guarantee a citation. See our [AI search citation guide](/blog/how-to-get-cited-by-chatgpt-and-google-ai-overviews) for more checks.

## How can you tell if one page needs a fix or the whole site needs a redesign?

**Test one service from search result to confirmed inquiry before deciding what to rebuild.** Search impressions, page visits, and qualified inquiries tell you different things. Find the first step where the path breaks, fix it, and test again before paying to change the rest of the site.

1. **Check whether the right page shows up.** In the [Search Console performance report](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/17010961), filter by the service page and look at the queries, impressions, and clicks it gets. If irrelevant searches dominate, check whether the title and page clearly describe the job you take on.
2. **Try it on a phone.** Open the page from the same link a customer would use. Check whether the service, coverage, and button are obvious and whether the layout shifts while you try to tap. If real-user performance data is available, [web.dev’s loading benchmark](https://web.dev/articles/defining-core-web-vitals-thresholds) puts good Largest Contentful Paint at 2.5 seconds or less at the 75th percentile. A good load time doesn’t tell you whether the form works.
3. **Finish the test inquiry.** Submit a clearly labeled test request. Look for a confirmation, make sure the right person receives it, and check what happens after hours. Track actual inquiries and booked jobs separately from phone taps and form starts.

## What else should Troy business owners know before changing their site?

### Can I target Troy if my business is based in another city?

Yes, if you actually serve Troy. Say you serve Troy rather than claiming to have a Troy office. Explain which services you provide there and how customers can check coverage. Do not create a Google Business Profile for an address where your business does not operate.

### Should I put a phone number or a quote form first?

Put the phone number first for urgent work if someone can answer it during the hours you advertise. Put a short quote form first for planned jobs that need details before scheduling. Make it clear when a request is received but an appointment is not yet confirmed.

### Should my Troy Google Business Profile link to my homepage?

Use the page that best represents the business or the location on that profile. If you operate a Troy office and have a dedicated location page with accurate hours, directions, and booking, that page may be more useful than a homepage that describes several offices.

### Should I fix one page or redesign my Troy website?

Fix one page first if the rest of the site works and you can isolate the problem. Consider a redesign when multiple pages share the same broken booking flow, hard-to-use mobile layout, or other problems you cannot fix within the current setup.
